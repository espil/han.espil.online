Centre - The System

The System Behind Espil.Online

Espil Online is a free knowledge-sharing community in a living world on real cities: a 3D player, Google Maps, and real businesses in one place. This page is its documentation and its implementation case study, arranged the way the world itself is arranged. The system sits at the centre. The story is to the left, the characters to the right, AI production above, and the houses below.

Product
A community and free knowledge-sharing solution in a 3D social world on real cities
Web build
espil.online - Three.js and Mapbox, social first, light UI, a simple male or female avatar
App & PC
The game build on 3D assets: quests, the exchange, and Zealandia, the future game land
Backend
Oracle Cloud Always Free, Fastify, Google sign-in, backend-issued sessions
Budget law

Governance
Written design laws and tokens in the production code; one loader standard on two production sites

What is implemented

The web build is live at espil.online. You arrive above a real city and dive the last stretch into it, then walk streets rebuilt from the city's own map data. Vancouver and Auckland stand today. Inside, the world begins as a mall: everything in every real store on the map can be traded, person to person, on trust, with no fee - promises, in an era of credit. The stall system (sellers and seekers, in real time, face to face) is the form that trade takes, and a guide walks new arrivals through the full journey to Auckland.

The app and PC program are the game build of the same world, on the same account. Character selection starts there, the game character is a server record created there, and Zealandia - the sunken land under New Zealand, entered only through the Auckland portal - is its first game expansion. The web stays light and social on purpose; the game weight lives in the app.

The backend, documented

Accounts are Google-only at this step, on a multi-provider identity structure so more providers can join without a rebuild. Sessions are issued by the backend, not the provider: an opaque token, hashed in the database, carried in an httpOnly cookie on espil.online, sliding for 30 days with a 90-day cap. Visitors who never sign in keep browsing as ghosts; the strict signup is deliberate, because trust is the foundation the trade runs on. Places data is served from the API on Oracle with a cache-aside store, so the map stays fast without paying for calls. The whole backend runs on Oracle Cloud Always Free: $0 recurring is a design constraint, not an accident.

Laws, tokens, one component family

The design system is a set of documented laws - palette, typography, interaction rules - written before implementation. Tokens are CSS custom properties in the production code itself, so a token change is the design decision and its implementation in the same act. Surfaces are composed from one reusable family (HUD, cards, buttons, loaders) before anything new is invented. The governance test already ran: one glass-loader implementation, built once against the laws, became the single standard on both production sites with no per-site redesign. The asset-size standard works the same way - sprite and 3D budgets, compression requirements, and per-place download targets, written before assets are produced.

Move with the arrow keys, the keycaps, the doors above, or by holding the mouse at a screen edge. Up and Down scroll the room first and turn at the end. Escape returns here.

Left - The Story

The story, and where it is written

Left is the past, and the past of this world is a story before it is a product. It is written as a novel, published on the Naver blog, and the game is built on top of it - not the other way around.

Espil, spelled backward

The name comes from my design house, Bieclipse. Spell it backward and you get "espil ceib"; I removed the "o" because o is earth. Espil Ceil is the maker at the centre of the story: a cauldron maker. The Cauldron is the space itself, entered through espil.online, and the World in the Cauldron is everything inside it - real cities, the people who walk them, and the knowledge they share. Inside the world, models and knowledge trade for Leo Coin, and the coin's supply grows through knowledge sharing, so the currency of the world is learning itself.

It began with a house

Before the world, there was a house I could not stop designing: 3D-printed, minimalist, set in nature without disturbing it, powered by its own hydroelectric and solar, with a VR chamber inside that copies a place completely. I never built that house in the real world. It became the seed of everything else - the houses stand below this room, and the world grew around them.

The canon, briefly

The novel is the canonical storyline, and the game follows it. Espil makes the cauldron the world lives in. Lunaris, its keeper, is a goddess who can stop time. Leo makes the coin. Hermes, a giant who is never aggroed, wanders the world carrying the key to the Time Stone. Two masters - Ahi of fire and Huka of ice - combine bound to the Time Stone and shatter with it, and Firzen is the name of that unborn fused power, not a person: mid-story, a fragment of the stone clings to Espil's hand, cold and heat at once like dry ice, and the power is absorbed into him. The Auckland Time Machine quest - the stone's fall, the touch, the crack revealed - opens the only portal to Zealandia and its first town, Waitematā.

The voice

On the blog, Espil Ceil writes the way the world thinks: poetic and philosophical, in short aphoristic lines, addressing the reader directly. Beauty, resonance, and trust run through it - trust most of all, because a world built on promises needs it. That voice is the world's constitution before any of its code: the trade with no fee, the strict signup, the knowledge shared freely, are all the story's values implemented.

The Naver Blog - where the story is published

The story, the Espil Ceil writings, and the world's notes in Korean live on my Naver blog. The documentary film of the world, "The World is Listening", is on YouTube.

Right - Character Production

Characters, Plushy AI, and the Meshy API

Right is production and decision. Every character in the world comes through one pipeline: drawn first, built locally by Plushy AI, finished with the Meshy API, and shipped as an engine-ready model.

1 - SourceA character is designed as an image first: face, hair, outfit, proportions.
→
2 - Plushy AIMy local image-to-3D pipeline turns the image into a model on my own machine.
→
3 - Meshy APIRigging, animation clips, and retexture when only the look changes.
→
4 - Engine-readyOptimized GLB under the asset-size standard: decimated, compressed, verified.

Plushy AI: the local pipeline

Plushy AI is my own local, private alternative to the cloud 3D services - the image-to-3D pipeline I built so the world's assets are made on my machine, under my control. Its services run in Docker containers. It also produces the world's buildings: curated city sprite sets and 3D building remakes, including the Vancouver pack that dresses the live city.

The Meshy API: rig, animate, retexture

Where the API is strongest, I use it: a 24-joint rig on the Plushy model, animation clips applied and merged (idle, walk, run, and each character's specials), and - the rule for every existing character - appearance changes are made by retexture, not regeneration. New eyes or new hair never rebuild the body; the same model is retextured, so a look can change while the character, the rig, and the animations stay exactly who they were.

Monsters, as sprites

Not everything needs to be 3D. Under the asset-size standard, ordinary creatures are sprite sheets - generated, framed, and state-driven as billboards in the town - while full 3D is reserved for the characters who carry the story.

Up - AI-Assisted Production

Made with AI, against written laws

Up is dreams and tools. This world is built with AI at every stage - but the AI works inside the system: the laws, the tokens, and the standards are written first, and the tools generate against them.

The build workflow

Figma exploration first, then AI agents - primarily Claude Code - for prototyping, implementation, and refactoring. I edit the code myself and choose which language and tool each job gets; the agents are how one person builds at the speed of a team, and the written laws are how the output stays one product instead of wandering. Every department of this build - map, interface, assets, skills, music, quality - works the same way: a written spec, generated production, and my verdict on the result.

Generation at scale

Plushy AI generates the world's matter: the city sprite pack alone is 115 curated sprites, 82 of them buildings in the Vancouver set. Skill effects are generated as frame sets - six abilities in fire and six in ice, drawn as matched pairs so the two attunements read as one system. Monsters come as sprite sheets, the Time Stone and the Auckland street buildings as 3D, and every output passes the asset-size standard before it is allowed into the build.

Music, film, and the assistants

Each city carries its own theme, composed with AI and finished by ear. The animation series follows one pacing law: every cut shows a start, a change, and a result. And two local assistants live on my own machine beside the pipeline: Plushy AI for making, and Ollama - whose Espil makes his first appearance in the app, the world's own guide, running locally like everything else that matters here.

Down - House Design & Concept Art

The Espil House Collection

Down is the current state, and the ground everything stands on: the house. The collection below is the oldest work on this page - the design everything else grew from.

The design brief

One house, designed for years before it had a world: 3D-printed, minimalist, set in nature without disturbing it. It powers itself - hydroelectric and solar - so it owes the land nothing. Inside, a VR chamber copies a place completely, so the house can be anywhere it has visited. I have not built this house in the real world; it is a design ambition, and inside Espil Online it is a place - the models of the collection trade there for Leo Coin, and one day the houses are the world's housing layer, where your website is your house.

The collection

Five houses, modelled in full - walkable builds exist for the collection, and the renders below are its concept art: the Universe house and its grounds, drawn at the hours the world keeps. The spiral you see in the roof is the same spiral as my mark; the house was the logo before the logo was.
