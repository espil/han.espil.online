01 — Cover Story

Espil.Online

Espil.Online began in 2015, at Bieclipse Design House — my own design practice — and it has been growing ever since. Today it is a free knowledge-sharing community in a living world on real cities, run as a live, interactive 3D map at espil.online — a Three.js world of real cities where you can walk the streets, and shop, drive, fly and dive with friends. I studied tourism for exactly this: social channels try to share a country's culture, but I am convinced only going there truly shares it - walking its streets yourself. This world is built on that conviction. I design it, build it AI-assisted, and ship it end-to-end myself: engine, interface, pipeline, sound and channels.

It started with a single obsession: I wanted to build a house. The design I aimed at was a 3D-printed house - minimalist, standing in nature without disturbing it, powered by its own water and sun, off the grid entirely. And because a house you never leave could grow dull, I imagined a VR chamber inside it: a room that copies a place completely - its temperature, its humidity, its wind - until you feel you are standing there. Everything in that house would be 3D-printed, and the models for all of it bought inside an online world - this one - traded not in real money but in Leo Coin, a currency that grows as knowledge is shared.

The name I sign this world with is Espil Ceil. Spell Bieclipse backward and you get espil ceib — I removed the “o” from cei’b’, as o is Earth.

02 — Selected Works

Nine doors into the same world

Reviewing my design work? Start with the three case studies: Espil.Online, Discover Holidays, and Apex Dermatology.

  1. Espil.Online CASE STUDY

    An online web-based 3D social world for real-time shopping and trading, played in real cities: a live interactive 3D world built with Three.js and shipped end to end by me - engine, UI, pipeline, sound, channels. It begins as a mall where everything in real stores can be bought, and its trade is designed to run person to person on trust: stalls in the world, promises instead of fees, a signup kept a little strict to protect both. I design it, build it AI-assisted, and run it under a documented design system of laws and tokens. → The case study: designing the system, not the screens · The design system behind it

  2. HMC - Han Mobility Consulting

    Hired in sales at a Freedom Mobile franchise, I became the company's full-stack developer and its backend person. I migrated the entire Excel-based customer list - years of monthly sheets - into a real database, fully manageable through CRUD, on an intranet CMS with dashboards. A Python script read the old sheets by pattern recognition and generated clean CSVs; a JavaScript reader scanned the standard contract PDFs and filed price, name, and address straight into the database.

  3. Nohproblem Consulting

    The company's first and only employee when I started, I was its all-in-one: logo design, web design and development, social media marketing, ads, SEO, blogs, video editing and YouTube management, client management, and a landing service for immigration clients. I grew its YouTube channel to its first 1,000 subscribers - the videos I produced are still its most-watched, with over 500,000 views between them - and by the time I left, the team had grown to about ten people.

  4. Higgs Creative

    Headhunted by the company's founders - dermatology doctors and business owners - I tested healthcare software: electronic medical records, CRM tooling, and OCR. My other work there was design-to-code: a designer produced the Figma designs, and I converted them into production HTML with a Figma-to-HTML tool, then wrote Python to merge the tool's similar class names into shared ones - an early, manual form of what design-to-code pipelines now automate. It took longer than building by hand, and it taught me what AI-assisted coding would become.

  5. AIO - All-in-One Service

    One gate, five directions. AIO is arranged by my five-direction design: split the sections so each reads as a page of its own, and a catalogue opens into branches of choices instead of a pile of buttons. The centre is the main page; the left is the past, and freedom; the right is the future, and the decision; the top is dreams, and the tools for them; the bottom is the current state. Inside, the Production wing runs the complete Kuro Sushi menu with a working cart, and holds the Sushi Umi kitchen recipe system in its format. → Enter AIO

  6. Discover Holidays CASE STUDY

    At a travel wholesaler, I rebuilt how the pricing catalogues are made. I converted a legacy PHP-era database into an API as a single source of truth, authored the style guide the catalogues are formatted by and enforced it with InDesign scripts across thousands of pages and hundreds of thousands of prices, and cut production work estimated at months down to a single day. The work converted my internship into a full-time role, and my tariffs carried the company's Alaska and Canada programs. It is also where I learned when not to ship: I built the online replacement within weeks, and chose not to launch it. → The case study: rebuilding the tariff system

  7. Zealandia

    Coming soon - the game app of Espil.Online: a 2D side-scrolling adventure in development, a game dungeon set on Zealandia, New Zealand's hidden land, the sunken continent beneath the sea. → The game extension of Espil.Online.

  8. Plushy AI + Ollama

    Two local AI systems that work beside me. Plushy AI is my own image-to-3D pipeline, built to generate 3D assets on my own hardware instead of renting the problem away. Buildings are a first-class output: city sets of building sprites and 3D remakes come out of this pipeline. Its services run in Docker containers, so the whole pipeline is reproducible: the same environment on any machine, every run. Ollama is where Espil - the world character, and my assistant - gets its character. Both exist because the world I am building needed tooling that did not exist off the shelf.

  9. Onggijonggi

    A non-profit Korean musical company in Vancouver, run like an entertainment house and still going strong. Its members have been picked up for background casting again and again, and for two seasons I was its marketing manager: promotion, member recruitment, and special events, including a live sports screening at the rented Korean community hall. On stage I performed in leading roles in its yearly small-theatre musicals, and our original production was covered by YTN.

04 — Capabilities

Seven departments, one person

AWeb & Interface

I design and build responsive websites and interfaces end-to-end — from first sketch to shipped page — in HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with React and Three.js/WebGL where the work calls for them.

BDesign & Visual

Twenty years of visual craft: brand, layout, 2D and 3D. My high-school designs were already in real use; my clinic, restaurant and retail clients still run on systems I designed.

CFull-Stack & Data

Backend runs through my whole career. At HMC (Han Mobility) I migrated an entire Excel customer list into a database with full CRUD, and built the company's intranet on the MERN stack. QDerm's booking ran on a PHP/MySQL database I managed. My headline workflow. I build with AI assistance — directing the tools, reviewing and editing the code myself, and choosing the right stack for each job. It is how Espil.Online ships, and how I built Plushy AI, my own local image-to-3D pipeline, run in Docker.

Operations work taught me where hours actually go. At Discover Holidays I wrote Python tooling on the intranet API and macros that cut a months-long job to one day — accuracy first, then speed, in that order.
